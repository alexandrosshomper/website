# Knauf Order Overview — Case Study
> By Alexandros Shomper | https://www.alexandrosshomper.de/portfolio/knauf-order-overview

## A unified platform for all Knauf orders

The single source of truth for deliveries from all Knauf brands and subbrands.

## Overview

- **Company:** Knauf Digital — innovation arm of Knauf Group (€15B+ global building materials leader, 43,500+ employees, 90+ countries)
- **Product:** Centralized track-n-trace platform for a fragmented conglomerate
- **Role:** Product Design Manager, 12 markets
- **Team:** 14 (PM, Engineering, Data, BA, Design)
- **Timeline:** 12 months
- **Stage:** 0 → 1 → n

## Key Results

- Inquiry Calls -20%
- Dealer Admin Work -2h/week
- Activation +30%
- Retention +25%
- Markets Rolled Out: 9

## The Problem

Construction dealers (B2B partners who sell Knauf materials and manage order logistics) managed thousands of orders across different Knauf entities. Delivery ETAs changed daily.

- Constantly changing ETAs
- No unified tracking platform
- Different contact points per Knauf entity
- Manual customer updates consuming hours weekly

"We spent more time chasing delivery info than selling materials."

**Constraints:** Every Knauf brand operated independently with different systems in each country. A brand wasn't just a sub-brand — in another market it was effectively a separate company. Scope changed frequently due to HQ reprioritisation. Several markets had built their own internal tools they relied on.

## Goal

Design a unified, easy-to-use order management platform that:
- Provides a unified overview of all orders' live status and changes
- Gives transparency to deliveries at material level
- Helps customers work more seamlessly together

## Research

Led field visits and interviews across 9 countries. Combined JTBD analysis, expert interviews, and a survey with 20 dealers in Europe.

**Key insights:**
- Changes in ETA = #1 priority
- Dealers' notification preferences vary widely
- Users needed fast setup (Day 1 value)

**PMF Hypothesis:** "If dealers have transparency about their deliveries, they will be able to better manage their customers, delivery points, and construction sites; returning daily."

## Solution — Key Features

### Feature 1: Unified Order Table
Single source-of-truth consolidating all delivery information across all Knauf entities.

- All Knauf entities in one view (10+ brands previously requiring separate contacts)
- Customizable columns so every customer sees what matters to them
- Simplified visual status system: standardized and color/icon-coded across all entities

**Impact:** 2h average saved per week · 20% reduction in service calls · 3x less back-to-back calls

### Feature 2: Calendar View
Bird's-eye view of upcoming deliveries displayed as calendar events.

- Visual timeline of the week
- Easy day planning for warehouse teams
- Helps manage fleet, warehouse, and logistics without clicking through orders

**Impact:** 25% reduction of logistics chokepoints · 2x warehouse planning efficiency

### Feature 3: Notification Settings
Personalized, granular update preferences.

- Choose specific events to be notified about
- Select channel per event: email, app, or SMS alerts
- Each customer has different preferences — maximum configurability

**Impact:** 2x improved change response time

### Feature 4: Share Order Details
Allow dealers to share live delivery info directly with applicators.

- One link, always up to date
- Reduced double communication

**Impact:** 45% less "where's my order?" calls from clients

## Broader Impact

Components built for the order table and calendar view were adopted across all four Knauf products, cutting delivery time by 40% portfolio-wide. Order Overview proved that transparency, done with precision, scales.

## Reflection

The calendar view should have been the starting point, not the second feature. Mental models clearly showed that logistics is time-first, not data-first — yet the order table was built first because it was the logical data structure.

On notification settings: maximum configurability from day one was right in principle, but choice paralysis delayed time-to-first-value. Smarter defaults with opt-in customisation would have served both goals better.
